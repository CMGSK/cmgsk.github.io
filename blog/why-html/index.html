I hate the new web

Why I wrote this web in plain HTML and how unnecessary it was to use a framework.

Jesus fuck, I am so tired. No matter which part of the internet I visit, it is loaded to its tops with tons of javascript snippets, functionalities that no one asks for, nobody uses, and almost everyone I know hates.
Like seriously, does anyone at this point in the internet likes carousels? They're annoying. But it's not just that. I miss the old internet, where you had some plain HTML with an uncomplicated customization, and everything was clear and simple.

I may be an old man yelling at clouds, but let me explain my point. I decided to do plain HTML/CSS on this website for a simple reason. Frameworks seem an overengineering thing to do for a personal website. The setup, the boilerplate, the amount of features they introduce that I know I will not use...
I don't want a lot of useless effects taking place on my website. Maybe it's because I'm a backend person, but I prefer the simplicity; just transmitting what I want in almost plain text.

And I get it. A lot of people are frontend developers, or work by doing impressive websites for big corporations and fancy companies that want their customers to enter their website and say "woah!", but there's a catch on that. There's too many people nowadays that are not good at doing reactive things, animations, and fancy frontend stuff. Seriously. I'm sorry if I'm too blatant about this, but it's the truth, I don't have enough fingers to count how many websites annoy me with poorly implemented effects.
From big corporations like Banco Santander with their hover buttons to open menus, or Twitter (yes, it's still Twitter to me) with the volume button on their videos displaying the volume on hover, but not adjusting the width to fit the actual width of the slider, to small websites and portfolios from independent developers being full of unsanitized things, ugly gaps, buttons that do nothing and the like, the actual web is fully loaded with frameworks that are nice for building enterprise-grade stuff, but unnecessary for a lot of things, and doing personal websites I think is one of them.

And I don't even want to mention Javascript. That is one hideous programming language I really do hate, but don't start me on that. I'll rant about it another day.

So why the rant?

Yeah, why I wrote all of this? Well, because.
And also to tell you why I chose to write this website in plain HTML, as the title says. I didn't think I needed any kind of framework to do the boilerplate for me, or serve my web, or create my buttons and define my styles. I can do it myself. And it will look nice, or at least that's my intention. I can just open a file and start writing a language I know without thousands of lines of Javascript defining how my web looks and behaves.

This web just consists of some folders, a CSS file, and plain text HTMLs. Each entry or section you click is a different, static file that I wrote myself, and that I modify in my PC and then push to my repository. No runtime, no hooks, no dynamic content, no database, just a fast loading, lightweight, plain text interpreted by your machine in the most simple way.

So please. I'm not saying you shouldn't use AI, I'm the first who uses it, but if you're vibecoding your web, trying to show off by using a complicated framework to then let an LLM do everything for you, at the very least, try to make it look different, read the code and see if there's something ugly going on, take a look around and see if something is broken... just try to avoid it looking a sloppy mess.
At least if I fuck it up, it'll be my fault, and it's not because I didn't understand what I was doing, it's because I'm an idiot who hates frontend.
